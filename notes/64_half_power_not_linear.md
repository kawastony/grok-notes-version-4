# Half-power is not in the linear class

**Date:** 2026-10-08
**Status:** Closure checked. Midpoint not promoted. Two identifications not forced.

Outside a linear source, the radial first integral is r phi' = C, and C scales with enclosed mass if J is linear in density. Then g = phi' scales as M/r, so v^2 scales as M. At M=4 the linear speed-squared is twice the baryonic Tully-Fisher value.

The paper law is v_inf = (Lambda_*^2 G M mu)^(1/4), so v^2 scales as sqrt(M) and g scales as sqrt(M)/r. Same 1/r shape. Different amplitude. A massless radial field has the shape and the wrong power. A massive one has neither.

Any linear radial deformation, A(r) phi'' + B(r) phi' + C(r) phi = J, with J linear in density and coefficients independent of M, stays in this class. Deficit, mass, chosen warp, and a midpoint term kappa (M_h[phi] - phi) are in it. Constant h only shifts the radial exponent off 1. Free h(r) can fit a profile. Neither produces sqrt(M).

The one reading that leaves the class is definitional. The midpoint identity says a symmetric spread h produces a depth h^2. If depth tracked M and the galaxy felt the spread, h = sqrt(M) and v^4 would track M. The cone metric does not force the field to be the spread rather than the depth. The matter coupling in the papers does not force depth proportional to M. Both would be selected. The half is not earned.

No midpoint ansatz was added to the equation.
