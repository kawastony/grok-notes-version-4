# Continuum Callias and the lattice operator

**Date:** 2026-10-05
**Status:** Investigation. No new diagonalization. Closed branches stay closed.

## Continuum

Callias, 1978, as restated by Gesztesy and Waurick, arXiv:1506.05144. On odd-dimensional R^n,

L = Q + Phi,

Q the Dirac operator, Phi Hermitian, |Phi| >= c > 0 outside a ball, derivatives decaying. Then L is Fredholm and Index(L) equals the degree of U = Phi/|Phi| on the sphere at infinity. In even dimensions the index vanishes for algebraic reasons. A unit hedgehog in three dimensions has degree 1.

Anghel, 1990, extends the same count to certain spin manifolds with a warped end, still with the mass invertible and radially constant at infinity. Poppitz and Unsal reduce an R^3 x S^1 index to Callias in the R^3 limit. None of these is a finite cube.

## Lattice

There is no lattice Callias theorem that says a Wilson hedgehog on an open box has index equal to shell degree.

What exists is different. Kubota, Ann. Henri Poincare 23, 1297 (2022), proves that the index of a twisted Dirac operator on the torus is recovered from the lattice Wilson-Dirac operator, by higher index theory of almost flat bundles. That is Atiyah-Singer on a closed manifold, not Callias on R^3. Hip, hep-lat/9712015, conjectures a lattice index as the difference of real eigenvalues of the Wilson operator weighted by chirality, again for gauge-field topology on a torus. Reinhardt and Tok, arXiv:0710.5445, find that a hedgehog of Wilson lines in a 3D slice of a 4D lattice contributes its winding to the overlap index. The defect there is a gauge-field texture, not a scalar mass on an open cube.

## What these runs were

The r=1 Wilson hedgehog, the overlap cut, and the no-Wilson ball are none of the three objects above. The continuum hypotheses fail: no sphere at infinity, no decay of derivatives outside a compact set, and on the naive lattice an eight-fold doubler kernel. The torus Wilson index was not computed. The gauge-hedgehog of Wilson lines was not built.

So the continuum theorem stands. The lattice theorem that exists does not apply to the tested boxes. The conjecture in note 27 is about those boxes only.
