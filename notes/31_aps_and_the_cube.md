# Atiyah-Patodi-Singer, and the finite cube

**Date:** 2026-10-05
**Status:** Investigation. No new diagonalization.

## The theorem

Atiyah-Patodi-Singer, 1975, is the index theorem for a Dirac operator on a compact manifold with boundary. The boundary condition is spectral: at the boundary, the spinor is projected onto the nonnegative eigenspace of the boundary Dirac operator. Under that condition the operator is Fredholm and

index = integral of A-hat ch minus one half of (eta(0) + h),

where eta is the eta invariant of the boundary operator and h is the dimension of its kernel. The eta term is the spectral asymmetry of the boundary. It is not optional. Local boundary conditions, Dirichlet or a missing neighbor, do not give this formula.

The cylinder form used in note 15 is the same theorem: the spectral flow of H(s) equals the index of partial_s + H(s) with APS conditions at the ends. That cylinder index was not computed. The windowed count was a substitute.

## The cube

An open finite cube with a hedgehog mass is not an APS problem as run. The boundary is six faces, not a sphere. The boundary condition was the absence of a hop, not the nonnegative spectral projection of a boundary Dirac operator. Eta of those faces was not computed. So APS does not assign the shell degree as the index of that operator.

Callias is the noncompact cousin: the boundary is sent to infinity and the mass is invertible there, so the eta piece becomes the degree. Anghel keeps an infinite warped end. Neither limit was taken.

## What the runs already said

On those cubes the spectral flow was an avoided crossing and the overlap index was 0. That is consistent with APS not applying, not a computation of the APS formula. A cube index would require the spectral boundary condition, or a proof that the open boundary is deformable to it. Neither is in the repo.
